# Under the Hood Constitution

## Core Principles

### I. Show, Don't Tell (Animation-First)
Every concept MUST be taught through an interactive figure that the learner touches and steps through. Text supports the figure and never replaces it. A lesson step MUST NOT carry more than about 60 words of text. If a concept can't be shown, the lesson isn't ready.

### II. Accurate, and Honest About Simplifying
Every lesson MUST be checked against at least one cited, authoritative source before release. Simplified models MUST be labelled as simplified ("real routers also do X"). Every lesson shows a "last reviewed" date and a "report an error" control. Confirmed errors MUST be fixed within 7 days.

### III. Touch-First, Works Everywhere
A phone with touch is the primary device. Every interaction MUST work by tap or drag, by keyboard, and with reduced motion (a step-through mode with no autoplay). A downloaded module MUST work fully offline. The app MUST run smoothly on a low-end Android phone (2 GB RAM) and load the first interactive figure in under 3 seconds on a 4G connection.

### IV. Learning That Sticks
Lessons MUST be short (7 minutes or less) and use active recall: predict-before-reveal on figures, questions straight after each lesson, and spaced review afterwards. Watching without doing is not a lesson.

### V. Respect the Learner
- No ads inside lessons, practice or review.
- Content released as free stays free.
- No dark patterns: no fake timers, no guilt-trip streak notifications, and cancelling is as easy as subscribing.
- Prices are shown clearly, in local currency.

### VI. One Library, Many Tracks
Core lessons are written once and are track-neutral. Tracks (Placement, Cert, Systems, School) add mapping, callouts and their own question banks on top of them. Tracks MUST NOT fork or duplicate core lessons.

### VII. Shareable by Design
Every figure MUST be shareable as a link that opens in a browser without installing anything, and exportable as a short video clip. Distribution is part of the product, not an afterthought.

## Content Standards
- One visual language for every figure: Hairline's isometric line style, one stroke weight, and one meaning per colour. A highlight always means "the thing to look at now".
- Every lesson follows the same shape: hook question, guided figure tour, predict step, free play ("break it" where it fits), recap, 3–5 questions, interview answer card.
- Plain international English, kept culture-neutral in core lessons. Region-specific examples go in track callouts.
- Copyright: we write original explanations. We never copy textbook passages, competitor content or exam questions word for word.

## Quality Gates
- No lesson ships without: a source check, a touch and keyboard pass, a reduced-motion pass, an offline pass, and a test on a low-end Android device.
- Accessibility: every figure has a text description, contrast meets WCAG 2.2 AA, and nothing depends on colour alone.
- Privacy: an account is optional for learning. Collect only the data needed for progress sync and purchases.

## Governance
This constitution overrides other project guidance. Any change needs a written reason, a version bump (semantic: MAJOR when a principle is removed or redefined, MINOR when one is added, PATCH for wording) and an update to any spec or plan it affects. Every spec and plan MUST include a constitution check.
